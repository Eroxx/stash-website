Stash Library · Mac, iPhone & iPad

Save it with one key. Stash takes it from there.

The bookmark app for web pages, videos and images. And it keeps track: what’s next in your queue, what you’ve opened, what you’re done with, and what just went on sale.

Saving is the easy part

If it’s on your screen, it’s one move from Stash.

A page, a video, a picture, a whole window of tabs. However you found it, there’s a quick way in.

  • ⌃⌘SAny web pageFrom Safari or Chrome, with the title already filled in. Pick a folder by typing.
  • ⌃⌘SAny YouTube videoThe same key on its page. It’s saved as a video, and plays inside Stash.
  • ⌃⌥⌘IPart of your screenDrag a rectangle over anything, in any app, and it’s saved as a picture.
  • DragAn image fileDrop it on Stash, or right-click it and choose Send to Stash.
  • ⇧SEvery tab at onceKeep a whole browser window as a session, or file it all and close it.
  • ShareFrom iPhone and iPadThe share sheet, from Safari, YouTube or any app, straight into a folder.
One library, three kinds

Everything you save, in one place.

Pages, videos and pictures share your folders and tags. Each has a tab of its own when you only want that kind.

  • Folders and tags, nested as deep as you like
  • A note on anything
  • Whole browser windows saved as sessions
  • Duplicates caught, dead links found
  • A Trash, and ⌘Z for everything
  • Snooze, or a reminder at a time
  • Five looks, light and dark

The queue

One queue for everything you want to get to next.

Articles and videos wait together in one list, in the order you choose, and it’s easy to change your mind. Add anything with one key, move it up or down with a click, or take it off again. Taking it off the queue never deletes it from your library.

  • Every item has its own ↑ ↓ and ✕ buttons, or use ⌥↑ ⌥↓ to reorder from the keyboard
  • Clear the whole queue in one go when you want a fresh start
  • Videos play right inside Stash; pages open in your browser
  • The Videos and Web tabs narrow it to just watching or just reading
Seen and archived

Finished with it? Archive it, don’t lose it.

Press a and a link folds away under Archived. It stays in its folder and every search still finds it, so your lists show what’s left and your library keeps everything.

  • Opened links are marked seen by themselves. No button to press
  • It keeps count, so what you saved and never opened is one view away
  • Archive a whole selection at once, or a new video straight from triage
Channels you follow

Your YouTube channels, as a to-do list instead of a feed.

Follow a channel by pasting its address, or straight from one of its videos. From then on its new uploads collect in one list, Videos to Triage, with a count beside each channel in the sidebar. Go through it whenever you like and decide on each video, or on the whole lot at once. When the list is empty, you’re caught up.

  • Keep a video and it’s saved to that channel’s folder, or put straight into your queue
  • One key per decision: q queue, m file, t tag, a archive, d skip. Or Save all and Skip all
  • Saved parts of a series (Part 3, Lesson 7) stay together, in order
  • Uses a free YouTube key that you set up once. Here’s how
Shopping lists, if you want them

Saving up for something? It watches the price.

Stash is a bookmark app first, so this stays out of the way until you ask for it. Call a folder Shopping, or switch on Shopping list from any folder’s menu, and the links in it get prices. Stash checks them again about once a week and shows the old price beside the new one when something gets cheaper.

  • Deals with a deadline count down. Stash reads the end date off the store’s page when the store lists one, as bundle stores usually do, and you can type a date in when it doesn’t. “Ends in 3 days” stands out as it gets close
  • Must have, Want and Someday, each with a running total
  • No shopping folder, no prices: you’ll never see any of it
One search box

Search the way you think.

Words, tags, sites, folders, how long a video runs, and whether you’ve opened it yet. ⌃⌘F from any app.

  • #tag site: folder: dur:<10 unseen archived broken
  • Save any search as a smart collection. It sits in the sidebar with its count and fills itself as you save: “bread recipes I haven’t opened” or “videos under ten minutes”, always one click away
  • Or build one from rules, no search syntax needed: folder, tag, words in the title or address, channel, length, when you saved it, how often you’ve opened it, seen or watched
  • In the Videos or Web tab, a collection shows just its videos or just its pages

Filing that does itself

It knows what you already own.

Keep a folder of notes, one for each thing you own: your camera, your Dutch oven, your standing desk. Point Stash at it once. From then on, whenever you save something about one of those things, a review, a tutorial, a recipe, Stash tags it with the thing itself. Everything about your Dutch oven ends up together, however long ago you saved it.

  • Any folder of Markdown notes: from Obsidian, or anywhere else
  • It asks before trusting a name that could mean something else, and remembers your answer
  • Leave the folder unset and none of this appears
Works with your AI app

Ask your AI what’s in your Stash.

Turn on one switch and your AI app can search your library, see your queue and check your shopping list. Stash speaks MCP, the open standard for connecting AI apps to your own tools, so it works with AI apps on your Mac that support it: Claude, ChatGPT’s Mac app, Cursor, VS Code, Raycast and more. Settings sets up Claude in one click.

  • Saving and filing only if you allow it. Nothing can ever be deleted
  • Off until you turn it on, and only for apps on this Mac you give the key to. Websites, including the browser versions of these apps, can’t reach it

On the Mac, two ways

A sidebar while you browse. A full app when you sort.

Stash docks to the edge of the screen beside your browser and moves the browser over to make room. Pin it to keep it open, or let it slide away when you’re done; ⌃⌘L brings it back from any app. When you want room to sort, ⌘L opens the same library as a full window, like any other app.

  • Works with Safari and Chrome, reading only the address and title of the tab you’re on
iPhone and iPad

Your library in your pocket, too.

On iPhone and iPad, Stash works alongside Stash on your Mac: the same library, kept in step through your own iCloud. Share anything into it from Safari, YouTube or any app, and it lands in the folder you pick.

  • The same folders, tags, queue and shopping lists you set up on the Mac
  • iPad gets a layout of its own, not a stretched phone
Why I made Stash

I bet you have more than one place where you save things. A bookmarks folder full of articles, a "Watch Later" list on YouTube, a camera roll of screenshots you promised yourself you'd look at again.

Mine held all of that and more: guitar tones, synth tutorials (hundreds of them), recipes, things I might buy someday. Over the years I tried, and paid for, more bookmark apps than I'd like to admit. Many of them have fantastic features, but I still wanted more, and I still needed a different app for each kind of thing I saved: one for articles, another for videos, something else for pictures.

So I built the one I wanted. One place for web pages, videos and pictures, with a queue for what I want to get to next, an archive for what I've finished, and a library that remembers what I've already seen.

That's Stash. I use it every day, and I hope you will too.

Eric

Your links, on your Mac, in a file you can read.

Stash keeps your library on your own machine as plain, readable JSON. No account to make, and no server in the middle. What goes out is the pages Stash fetches to get you a title and a price, and, only if you connect one, what you ask your AI app about. If you ever stop using Stash, your links are still right there.
